### 1.4 Gaps

1. **Five category labels, no single one.** "Legal intelligence platform" (JD), "legal AI action layer" (Series B), "Legal AI for In-house Counsel" (site), "AI workspace" ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/gc-ai/)), "leading legal AI platform for companies of all sizes" (JD). A PMM JD that asks to "Connect product messaging to GC AI’s broader Legal AI category narrative" needs one name first
2. **The in-house wedge is no longer unique.** Harvey, Legora, LegalOn and Spellbook all now claim in-house buyers (1.2). The core message still leans on "not built for law firms," which buyers now hear from everyone
3. **Proof point drift.** Customer counts in public copy range 1,000 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251107988263/en)), 1,200 ([vs Harvey](https://gc.ai/compare/gc-ai-vs-harvey)), 1,500 ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/gc-ai/reviews)), 1,700 and 2,000 ([llms.txt](https://gc.ai/llms.txt)), 2,100 (JD). GC AI's own llms.txt asks AI tools to prefer newer numbers, which confirms the drift is known
4. **Price objection is unanswered.** $500 per seat is public and competitors attack it against ChatGPT Business. No public "why not ChatGPT" ROI page answers it in a lean team frame, though a [vs ChatGPT page](https://gc.ai/compare/gc-ai-vs-chatgpt) exists
5. **Proof is self reported.** Bench, ROI study and NPS are all GC AI generated. Review volume (9 on G2, 0 on Capterra) is low for 2,100 customers. Third party proof is the cheapest credibility win
6. **Expansion story is invisible.** The Demand Gen JD says teams bring in "colleagues outside legal." Public messaging is still lawyer only, so the expansion motion has no narrative
